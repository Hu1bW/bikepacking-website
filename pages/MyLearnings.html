I grew a lot as a person because of this hobby

Up until recently, I had never really traveled alone. I had always lived in comfort zones, surrounded by people and places I was familiar with. The decisions I would make were the save ones and I thought I was content with that kind of life. As i got older though, I started to be more greedy in what I wanted my days to look like and wondered what else was out there and what I was missing out on.

My first journey was a 5 week long trip to Slovenia, alone. I had no real idea or plan and my goal was to learn to live in the moment and be comfortable with being uncomfortable. I learned much more than that though, I learned that I was indeed capable of living on the road. The obstacles were there for sure but in time learned to life by the day and to do the things that I wanted to do and nothing else.

There is always a solution to a problem and the problems that are thrown at you are much bigger in our head that they actually are.

The things is, it's not that far actually!

The more you enjoy your work, the easier it is to do it. Before setting out on my first trip, I was worried about the distance I would have to cover each day. I thought it would be exhausting and hard and I would have to be at a certain place at a certain time each day.

At firt this held some truth to it, I was not used to riding a bike for more than a couple of hours a day. But as the days went by, I learned that I enjoyed the journey more that the destination. This, combined with a increasingly more layed back mindset, made the distances seem much smaller. I learned to enjoy the ride and the scenery and the people I met along the way. The distances became less of a concern and more of a challenge to be met.

I learned to love traveling by bike

I will be the first to admit that bikepacking falls under type-2 fun. It is not always easy and it can be hard work. But the freedom that comes with traveling by bike is so much better than any other form of travel. You can stop wherever you want, whenever you want. You can take detours and explore new places without having to worry about anything but finding some food or a place to put you tent.

It feels weird to say it, because I am not a very hippy person, but traveling by bike feels like your journey and your destination melt into one. You are in the moment and you are doing what you want to do. There is no rush, no stress, just you and your bike and the road ahead of you.

But yea climbing hills hurts. And there are a lot of hills out there.

This first trip alone was mentally kind of tough

As someone who has a rhythm to their life and has a set amount of people in my circle, I found the first few days of my trip to be really tough. I had not thought about it, but I had never really been alone in this way before. Where everything was up to me and I had no one to fall back on. The first few days were filled with what I can only describe as withdrawal symptoms. I felt anxious, overwhelmed and without purpose. I was already making compromises in my head about the challenge that I had set for myself and I was not even a week in.

But as with all withdrawal symptoms, they eventually passed and I felt a little bit better about myself. Not as much as I would have liked in the moment however. A good two weeks into the trip, I put myself in a rhythm and just lived bu the day. Wasn't really enjoying myself but was kind of doing a constant chore.

I thought a lot about a lot of things during this time. And the questions I asked myself were never really happy ones. And the one thing I learned in this trip in particular, is that you wont get answers no matter how much mental effort you put into it. These answers are meant to come from other people and other experiences.

In the end I made it to Slovenia, can't say I had the time of my life but I am proud that I made it and I learned a lot about myself in the process.

Also learned what not to do or worry about on my next trip.
